## Task 1: Getting Started with Photoshop: Exploring the Workspace (3 Points)

<details markdown="1">

<summary>👉Click to expand the step by step guide</summary>

### Objective
In this task, you will learn to navigate and customize the Photoshop interface by configuring panels, tools, and workspace themes. A well organized workspace improves efficiency and helps you access tools quickly.

### What You'll Learn
- How to show/hide panels
- How to dock and arrange panels
- How to customize the interface theme
- How to save a custom workspace

### Background & Resources
Before starting, familiarize yourself with Photoshop's interface:
- **Adobe's Official Guide:** [Photoshop Workspace Basics](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/using/workspace-basics.html)
- **Video Tutorial:** Search YouTube for "Photoshop Workspace for Beginners"
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Panels** are windows that contain tools and options (Layers, Properties, etc.)
  - **Docking** means attaching panels together or to the interface edges
  - **Workspace** is a saved arrangement of panels and tools

### Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Open a blank document**
1. Launch Adobe Photoshop
2. Go to **File → New**
3. Choose any preset (e.g., "Default Photoshop Size")
4. Click **Create**

**Step 2: Enable required panels**
1. Go to **Window** menu (top menu bar)
2. Check (enable) the following panels:
   - ✓ Layers
   - ✓ Properties
   - ✓ Actions
   - ✓ Color
3. These panels will now appear in your workspace

**Step 3: Configure the Tools panel**
1. Locate the **Tools panel** (vertical bar on the left with tool icons)
   - If the Tools panel is not visible, go to **Window → Tools** to enable it

   ![Tools Panel](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dipaish/dipaish/refs/heads/main/images/courseRelatedImages/laurea/adobe/panel.png)

2. Click the **double arrow icon** (⋮⋮) at the top of the Tools panel
3. Select **2 Column Layout**

**Step 4: Dock Actions panel**
1. Click and hold the **Actions panel tab** (at the top where it says "Actions")
2. Drag it slowly to the **left side** of the screen, next to the Tools panel
3. As you drag near the docking area, you should see:
   - A **blue highlight** or **thin blue line** indicating where the panel will dock
   - Or a **semi-transparent preview** of the panel position
4. Release the mouse when you see the docking indicator
5. The Actions panel should now be docked next to Tools
   - If it didn't dock, try again by dragging more slowly and positioning it very close to the edge of the Tools panel

**Step 5: Dock Color panel**
1. Click and hold the **Color panel tab**
2. Drag it slowly **below the Actions panel** on the left side
3. Look for the **blue highlight** or **preview line** showing where it will dock
4. Release when you see the docking indicator
5. Color panel should now be stacked under Actions
   - If it didn't dock properly, drag it again and position it directly under the Actions panel

**Step 6: Change interface theme**
1. Go to **Edit → Preferences → Interface** 
2. Under **Appearance**, select the **second gray option** (Medium Gray)
3. Click **OK**

**Step 7: Save your workspace**
1. Go to **Window → Workspace → New Workspace...**
2. Name it: `YourFull Name Workspace`
3. The **Capture** section shows that panel locations will be saved automatically
4. Optionally check any of the following (recommended to check all):
   - ✓ **Keyboard Shortcuts**
   - ✓ **Menus**
   - ✓ **Toolbar**
5. Click **Save**

**Step 8: Take a screenshot**
1. Make sure your entire Photoshop window is visible, including:
   - All docked panels (Tools, Actions, Color on the left; Layers, Properties on the right)
   - Your workspace name should be visible at the top of the window
   - The gray theme is applied
2. Capture the full Photoshop interface
3. Save as: `Task01_Workspace_FirstName_LastName.png`

**Sample answer for reference:**

![Sample Workspace Setup](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dipaish/dipaish/refs/heads/main/images/courseRelatedImages/laurea/adobe/sampleAnswer.png)

Save in your **Task01_Workspace** folder:
- `Task01_Workspace_FirstName_LastName.png` (screenshot showing your customized workspace)

💡 If you make a mistake, go to **Window → Workspace → Reset Essential** to start over  
💡 You can access your saved workspace anytime via **Window → Workspace → Your Workspace Name**

## Task 2: Crop & Resize for Different Outputs (3 Points)

<details markdown="1">
<summary>👉Click to expand the step by step guide</summary>

### Objective
Learn how to properly crop, straighten, and resize images for different output formats (social media and print). Understanding resolution and dimensions is essential for producing professional quality images.

### What You'll Learn
- How to use the Crop Tool and straighten images
- The difference between pixel dimensions and resolution (PPI)
- How to resize images for web vs. print
- How to export images in appropriate formats

### Background & Resources
Before starting, understand these key concepts:
- **Adobe's Official Guide:** [Crop and Straighten Photos](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/using/crop-straighten-photos.html)
- **Video Tutorial:** Search YouTube for "Photoshop Crop and Resize for Beginners"
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Pixel dimensions** (e.g., 1080×1080) determine the actual size of the image
  - **Resolution (PPI)** = Pixels Per Inch; 72 ppi for web, 300 ppi for print
  - **Aspect ratio** (e.g., 1:1, 16:9) is the proportional relationship between width and height
  - **Instagram posts** use 1080×1080 pixels (1:1 square format)
  - **Print images** require higher resolution (300 ppi) for sharp output

### Practice Image
Use: `landscape.jpg` from the **Task02_CropResize** folder

### Step-by-Step Instructions

**Part A: Create Instagram Version**

**Step 1: Open the image**
1. Go to **File → Open**
2. Navigate to **Task02_CropResize** folder
3. Select `landscape.jpg`
4. Click **Open**

**Step 2: Select the Crop Tool**
1. Click the **Crop Tool** in the Tools panel (or press **C**)
2. The crop overlay will appear on your image

   ![Crop Tool Location](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dipaish/dipaish/refs/heads/main/images/courseRelatedImages/laurea/adobe/cropTool.png)

**Step 3: Straighten the horizon**

The Straighten tool helps fix tilted or crooked images by rotating them to align horizontal or vertical elements (like horizons, buildings, or walls). This is essential for professional looking photos.

1. In the **Options Bar** at the top, click the **Straighten Tool** icon (looks like a level)
2. Click and drag along the horizon line in your image (the horizon can be anywhere in the photo: top, middle, or bottom)
3. Photoshop will automatically rotate the image to level it
4. Press **Enter** to apply

**Step 4: Set aspect ratio for Instagram**
1. Select the **Crop Tool** again (**C**)
2. In the **Options Bar**, click the dropdown that says "Ratio"
3. Select **1:1 (Square)**
4. Adjust the crop box to frame your subject nicely
5. Press **Enter** to apply the crop

**Step 5: Resize for Instagram**
1. Go to **Image → Image Size**
2. Make sure **Constrain Proportions** (chain link icon) is locked
3. Set **Width** to `1080` pixels
4. **Height** should automatically set to `1080` pixels
5. Set **Resolution** to `72` Pixels/Inch
6. Make sure **Resample** is set to **Automatic**
7. Click **OK**

**Step 6: Export Instagram version**
1. Go to **File → Export → Export As...**
2. In the Export dialog, configure:
   - **Format:** JPG
   - **Quality slider:** 6 or 7 (toward High) for good quality with reasonable file size
3. Click **Export**
4. Save the file
   - **Save location:** Your **Task02_CropResize** folder
   - **File name:** `Task02_Instagram_FirstName_LastName.jpg`

---

**Part B: Create Print Version (A4 @ 300 PPI)**

**Step 7: Undo crops (start fresh)**
1. Go to **File → Revert** to reload the original image
2. Or press **Ctrl+Z** (Windows) / **Cmd+Z** (Mac) multiple times to undo

**Step 8: Straighten again**
1. Select **Crop Tool** (**C**)
2. Use **Straighten Tool** to level the horizon again
3. Press **Enter**

**Step 9: Crop for print (no constraint)**
1. With **Crop Tool** active, set the dropdown to **Original Ratio**
2. Crop out any unwanted edges or distractions
3. Press **Enter**

**Step 10: Resize for A4 print**
1. Go to **Image → Image Size**
2. Change units to **Centimeters** (from dropdown)
3. Set **Resolution** to `300` Pixels/Inch
4. Set **Width** to `21` cm (A4 width) or **Height** to `29.7` cm (A4 height)
   - **Landscape orientation** (image wider than tall): Set Width to 21 cm (height will adjust automatically)
   - **Portrait orientation** (image taller than wide): Set Height to 29.7 cm (width will adjust automatically)
   - Check the current dimensions in the dialog to determine your image's orientation
   - The other dimension will scale proportionally since Constrain Proportions is locked
5. Make sure **Resample** is checked and set to **Automatic**
6. Click **OK**

**Step 11: Export print version**
1. Go to **File → Export → Export As...**
2. In the Export dialog, configure:
   - **Format:** JPG
   - **Quality slider:** 6 or maximum (all the way to High) for best print quality
   - 123Click **Export**
   - **File name:** `Task02_Print_FirstName_LastName.jpg`
   - **Save location:** Your **Task02_CropResize** folder


### Deliverables
Save in your **Task02_CropResize** folder:
- `Task02_Instagram_FirstName_LastName.jpg` (1080×1080 px, 72 ppi)
- `Task02_Print_FirstName_LastName.jpg` (A4 size, 300 ppi)

### Tips
💡 **Protect your original:** Keep a backup copy of the original image file in a separate folder before editing. Use **File → Revert** to reload the last saved version if you need to start over  
💡 Instagram accepts up to 1080×1080 for square posts. Larger files will be compressed  
💡 Use **Straighten Tool** for horizons, building edges, or any vertical/horizontal reference line  
💡 Higher quality % = larger file size but better image quality

## Task 3: Object Removal & Retouching (4 Points)
<details markdown="1">
<summary>👉Click to expand the step by step guide</summary>

### Objective
In this task, you will lean how to remove unwanted objects from a real urban street photo using Photoshop retouching tools while keeping the image realistic. You will practice professional **photo cleanup techniques** commonly used in advertising, architecture, and urban photography.

### What You'll Learn
- How to use the Remove Tool for quick object removal
- Working with the Spot Healing Brush for blemishes and small objects
- Using the Patch Tool for larger area replacement
- Nondestructive retouching with separate layers
- How to maintain realistic textures and backgrounds

### Background & Resources
Before starting, understand object removal techniques:
- **Adobe's Official Guide:** [Remove Objects from Photos in Photoshop](https://www.adobe.com/products/photoshop/remove-object.html)
- **Video Tutorial:** Search YouTube for "Photoshop Remove Tool Tutorial"
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Remove Tool** uses AI to intelligently remove objects and fill the background
  - **Spot Healing Brush** automatically samples surrounding pixels to blend repairs
  - **Patch Tool** lets you manually select a source area to cover unwanted elements
  - **Nondestructive editing** means working on separate layers to preserve the original

### Practice Image
Use: `cluttered_scene.jpg` from the **Task03_Retouching** folder

This image shows rental bikes cluttering a pavement which is a common urban photography problem where objects block visual space or distract viewers.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

### Step 1: Open & Save Working File
1. Open `cluttered_scene.jpg` in Photoshop.
2. Save immediately as: `Task03_Retouching_FirstName_LastName.psd`

### Step 2: Create a Retouch Layer
1. Open the **Layers Panel**
2. Click **New Layer**
3. Rename it: retouch
4. Select this layer before editing. All edits must happen on this layer.

**Step 3: Select and configure the Remove Tool**
1. In the **Tools panel**, locate the **Spot Healing Brush Tool**

   ![Remove Tool](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dipaish/dipaish/refs/heads/main/images/courseRelatedImages/laurea/adobe/removeTool.png)

   - Right-click (or click and hold) on the **Spot Healing Brush Tool** icon
   - A menu will appear showing grouped healing tools
   - Select **Remove Tool** from the menu (it looks like a brush with sparkles)
   - You can also press **Shift+J** repeatedly to cycle through all healing tools until you see "Remove Tool" in the tool name

2. In the **Options Bar** at the top, configure the tool settings:
   - Check (✓) **Sample All Layers**, this allows the tool to use information from all visible layers

3. Set your **Brush Size** appropriately:
   - Use **[** key to make brush smaller
   - Use **]** key to make brush larger
   - Start with a size slightly larger than the object you want to remove
   - You'll see a circular cursor showing the brush size.

**Step 4: Remove unwanted objects**
1. **Identify objects to remove**. You must remove at least **5 objects** such as:
   - Dockless bikes (complete bikes or parts like wheels, handlebars)
   - Street signs or poles
   - Pedestrians
   - Bollards or street obstructions
   - Shadows left by removed objects
2. **Zoom in** to 100-150% for better control (Ctrl/Cmd + Plus)
3. **Paint over each object** using the Remove Tool:
   - Simply click and drag to paint over the object you want to remove
   - Work gradually with smaller brush strokes rather than one large stroke
   - Let Photoshop process each removal before moving to the next (you'll see a progress indicator)
   - The AI will intelligently fill the area with matching background texture
4. **Repeat** for each of the 5+ objects you want to remove
5. **Zoom out** (Ctrl/Cmd + 0) periodically to check overall appearance

**Step 5: Repair pavement texture with Spot Healing Brush** (Optional)
1. After removing objects, you may notice gaps or imperfections in the pavement
2. Select the **Spot Healing Brush Tool** (press **J**)
3. In the **Options Bar**, set:
   - **Type:** Content-Aware
   - **Sample All Layers:** checked (✓)
4. **Click or brush over** small marks, edges, and minor texture issues
   - Use this tool for areas smaller than your palm when viewing at 100%
5. Adjust brush size as needed with **[** and **]** keys


**Step 6: Final realism check**
1. Toggle the visibility of your retouch layer on/off (click the **eye icon**)
2. Compare before and after versions
3. Ask yourself these questions:
   - Does anything look repeated or cloned?
   - Are shadows believable and consistent with the lighting?
   - Can someone easily tell objects were removed?
4. If you answer "yes" to the last question, refine problem areas:repeat the above steps until you are happy. 😊

**Step 9: Export final image**
1. Make sure all layers are visible
2. Go to **File → Export → Export As...**
3. Set **Format** to **JPG**
4. Set **Quality** to high 
5. Click **Export**
6. Save as: `Task03_Final_FirstName_LastName.jpg` in your **Task03_Retouching** folder

**Step 10: Save your PSD file**
1. Go to **File → Save As...**
2. Make sure the format is **Photoshop (*.PSD)**
3. Confirm the filename: `Task03_Retouching_FirstName_LastName.psd`
4. Save in your **Task03_Retouching** folder

### Deliverables
Save in your **Task03_Retouching** folder:
- `Task03_Retouching_FirstName_LastName.psd`
- `Task03_Final_FirstName_LastName.jpg` (final cleaned image)


### Tips
💡 **Work nondestructively:** Always retouch on a separate layer so you can undo changes without affecting the original  
💡 **Less is more:** Remove only truly distracting elements.Don't over-clean the scene.  
💡 If Remove Tool creates artifacts, try using smaller brush strokes or the Spot Healing Brush instead  
💡 For objects near complex backgrounds (trees, patterns), the Patch Tool often gives better control  
💡 **Zoom in and out** frequently to check your work at different scales

##  Task 4: Portrait Fixes (5 Points)

<details markdown="1">
<summary>👉Click to expand the step by step guide</summary>

### Objective
Learn essential portrait retouching techniques including red-eye removal, teeth whitening, and blemish cleanup. These are fundamental skills for professional portrait editing.

### What You'll Learn
- How to remove red-eye effect from flash photography
- Teeth whitening using adjustment layers and masks
- Blemish removal with healing tools
- Natural-looking portrait enhancements

### Background & Resources
Before starting, understand portrait retouching:
- **Adobe's Official Guide:** [Retouch and Repair in Photoshop](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/using/retouching-repairing-images.html)
- **Video Tutorial:** Search YouTube for "Photoshop Portrait Retouching for Beginners"
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Red-eye** is caused by camera flash reflecting off the retina
  - **Adjustment layers with masks** allow targeted, nondestructive edits
  - **Natural retouching** enhances without making the person look artificial
  - Over-whitening teeth can look fake—aim for natural improvement

### Practice Image
Use: `portrait_redeye.jpg` from the **Task04_RedEye** folder

### Step-by-Step Instructions

**Part A: Remove Red Eye**

**Step 1: Open the portrait image**
1. Go to **File → Open**
2. Navigate to **Task04_RedEye** folder
3. Select `portrait_redeye.jpg`
4. Click **Open**

**Step 2: Duplicate the background layer**
1. Right click the **Background** layer in Layers panel
2. Select **Duplicate Layer**
3. Name it `retouched`
4. Click **OK**

**Step 3: Select the Red Eye Tool**
1. In the Tools panel, click and hold the **Spot Healing Brush**
2. Select **Red Eye Tool**
3. In the **Options Bar**, set:
   - **Pupil Size:** 50%
   - **Darken Amount:** 50%

**Step 4: Click on red eyes**
1. **Zoom in** (Ctrl/Cmd + Plus) to see eyes clearly
2. **Click once** in the center of each red pupil
3. Photoshop will automatically correct the red-eye
4. If result isn't perfect, adjust **Pupil Size** and **Darken Amount** and try again

---

**Part B: Whiten Teeth**

**Step 5: Create Hue/Saturation adjustment layer**
1. In the **Layers panel** (bottom section), look for the **Adjustment Layer** icon at the bottom

   ![Adjustment Layer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dipaish/dipaish/refs/heads/main/images/courseRelatedImages/laurea/adobe/adjustmentlayerTask4.png)
   
   - It looks like a **half-filled circle** 
   - It's usually located at the bottom of the Layers panel, between the layer icons
2. Click on it and select **Hue/Saturation** from the menu
3. The adjustment layer appears above your retouched layer

**Step 6: Adjust yellows**
1. In the **Properties panel**, locate the row of **colored circles** just below the Preset dropdown
   - You'll see 7 colored circles: a multicolor circle, then red, yellow, green, cyan, blue, and magenta
   - These circles let you target specific color ranges in your image
2. **Click on the YELLOW circle** 
   - This tells Photoshop to only adjust yellow tones in the image
3. Now decrease the **Saturation** slider to around **-40 to -50**
   - Drag the slider to the left (toward negative values)
   - This removes yellow tones from teeth, making them appear whiter
4. You might also increase **Lightness** slightly (+5 to +10) if needed

**Step 7: Invert the mask**
1. In the **Layers panel**, look at the **Hue/Saturation 1** layer
   - You'll see two small thumbnails: the adjustment icon and a white rectangle (the mask)
2. **Click on the white rectangle** (mask thumbnail) to select it
   - A white border will appear around it when selected
3. Now invert the mask using either method:
   - **Method A:** Press **Ctrl+I**
   - **Method B:** In the **Properties panel** (Masks section), click the **Invert** button at the bottom
4. The mask turns **black** (you'll see it change in the Layers panel)
   - **What this does:** Black on a mask means "hide the effect"
   - Right now, the teeth whitening is affecting the ENTIRE image
   - By making the mask black, we hide the whitening effect everywhere
   - **Why we do this:** In the next step, we'll paint white ONLY on the teeth to reveal the whitening just where we want it

**Step 8: Paint on teeth**
1. Press **B** to select the **Brush Tool**
2. Set **Foreground color to White**
3. In **Options Bar**, set **Opacity** to **50-70%**
4. Set brush to **Soft Round** with appropriate size
5. **Paint over the teeth** to reveal the whitening effect
6. Be careful not to whiten the gums or lips

---

**Part C: Remove Blemishes**

**Step 9: Select the Spot Healing Brush**
1. In the **Layers panel**, **click on the `retouched` layer** to select it
   - This is important! Blemish removal should happen on this layer, NOT on the mask
2. Press **J** to select **Spot Healing Brush Tool**
3. In **Options Bar**, set:
   - **Type:** Content-Aware
   - **Size:** Slightly larger than the blemish

**Step 10: Remove 3-5 blemishes**
1. **Click or brush over** any skin blemishes, spots, or minor imperfections
2. Remove at least **3-5 blemishes**
3. Use short clicks rather than long strokes for better results
4. Adjust brush size as needed

**Step 11: Check your work**
1. **Zoom out** to normal view
2. Toggle layer visibility (click eye icon) to compare before/after
3. Make sure edits look natural but not over processed

---

**Step 12: Export images**

**Export the after image:**
1. Turn all layers back on to show final retouching
2. **File → Export → Export As**
3. Format: **JPG**, Quality: **8-9** (high quality)
4. Name: `Task04_After_FirstName_LastName.jpg`

**Save PSD file:**
1. **File → Save As**
2. Format: **Photoshop (*.PSD)**
3. Name: `Task04_Portrait_FirstName_LastName.psd`

### Deliverables
Save in your **Task04_RedEye** folder:
- `Task04_After_FirstName_LastName.jpg`
- `Task04_Portrait_FirstName_LastName.psd`

### Tips
💡 For red-eye, one click is usually enough. Don't click repeatedly  
💡 When whitening teeth, use **low opacity** (50-70%) for natural results  
💡 For blemishes, use a brush slightly **larger than the blemish** itself  

### Grading Criteria (5 pts)

| Criterion | Points |
|-----------|-------:|
| Red-eye completely removed from both eyes | 1 |
| Teeth whitened naturally (not over-processed or affecting gums) | 2 |

## Task 5: Color Correction (5 Points)
<details markdown="1">
<summary>👉Click to expand the step by step guide</summary>

### Objective
In this task, you will learn professional color correction techniques using Camera Raw Filter. You'll fix exposure problems, adjust white balance, and enhance colors while working nondestructively with Smart Objects.

### What You'll Learn
- Converting layers to Smart Objects for nondestructive editing
- Using Camera Raw Filter for comprehensive color adjustments
- Understanding exposure, highlights, shadows, and white balance
- Working with Tone Curves for advanced tonal control

### Background & Resources
Before starting, understand color correction:
- **Adobe's Official Guide:** [Adjust Color and Tone in Camera Raw](https://helpx.adobe.com/camera-raw/using/make-color-tonal-adjustments-camera.html)
- **Video Tutorial:** Search YouTube for "Photoshop Camera Raw Color Correction"
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Smart Objects** preserve original data and allow re-editable filters
  - **White Balance** corrects color temperature (warm/cool tones)
  - **Exposure** controls overall brightness
  - **Highlights** affect bright areas, **Shadows** affect dark areas
  - **Tone Curve** provides precise control over tonal ranges

### Practice Image
Use: `poorly_exposed.jpg` from the **Task05_ColorCorrection** folder
